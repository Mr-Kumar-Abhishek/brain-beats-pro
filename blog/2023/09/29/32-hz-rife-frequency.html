2. Clinical Applications: Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)

A. Pathophysiology of Macular Degeneration

Age-Related Macular Degeneration is the leading cause of irreversible central vision impairment in older adults:

  1. Dry (Atrophic) AMD: Characterized by the progressive accumulation of drusen between the RPE and Bruch’s membrane, chronic low-grade inflammation, and geographic atrophy of macular photoreceptors.
  2. Wet (Neovascular) AMD: Characterized by abnormal choroidal neovascularization (CNV) breaking through Bruch’s membrane, causing exudative fluid leakage, hemorrhage, and rapid fibrovascular scarring.
  3. Bio-Acoustic Strategy: Delivering 32 Hz frequency therapy supports dry AMD stabilization by enhancing cellular waste clearance, supporting antioxidant enzyme pathways, and maintaining microvascular oxygenation before irreversible atrophy occurs.
